What we're testing
We look at five simple rules: PCR (put-to-call ratio), market halves (is Nifty in the bottom, middle, or top third?), no-expiry days, same-day moves, and VIX zones. For each one, we ask: "If we saw this yesterday, would Nifty go up tomorrow?" We test on 3,000 random shuffles to see if the answer beats luck.

Our quality checks
We use three levels of rigor to avoid fooling ourselves: (1) Holm correction for multiple tests, (2) last 20 days excluded (they're noisy), (3) learn on first 60% of data, test on last 40% against a baseline of "always guess up." About this dashboard
This dashboard is a learning tool for Indian retail investors. It shows open interest patterns, options positioning, and trend analysis—not predictions. All data is educational; never invest based on a dashboard. The code and ideas are credited to Claude (Anthropic).

Reference: Claude Dashboard for Nifty Options Analysis (Anthropic, 2026). Web-based tool for educational analysis of NSE F&O data.
30-Second Summary
We track where traders are building their positions (open interest), what options positioning tells us (put-call ratio), and whether simple price patterns help us guess tomorrow's direction. All numbers are updated daily during market hours. This is research, not trading advice.
Glossary
Open Interest (OI)
In plain words: The number of open call and put contracts traders hold right now.
In this dashboard: Shows concentration by strike. Big OI at one strike = traders are betting there.
Say it like this: "Call OI at 24000 is high" = many traders own calls at 24000 strike.
Put-Call Ratio (PCR)
In plain words: How many put options (bet on down) vs call options (bet on up) traders hold.
In this dashboard: PCR > 1 = more puts; PCR < 1 = more calls.
Say it like this: "PCR is 1.2" = traders own 20% more puts than calls.
Support & Resistance
In plain words: Price levels where many traders placed bets.
In this dashboard: Support = strike with most put OI (downside bets). Resistance = strike with most call OI (upside bets).
Say it like this: "Resistance at 24500" = many traders own call options at 24500 strike.
Max Pain
In plain words: The price at expiry where the most traders lose money.
In this dashboard: Shows what price would hurt the most positions at once.
Say it like this: "Max pain at 24100" = if Nifty closes at 24100 on expiry, most traders lose.
India VIX
In plain words: Market fear. Higher = traders think tomorrow is riskier.
In this dashboard: Used to forecast range (±1σ, ±2σ).
Say it like this: "VIX is 18" = market is calm; VIX is 25 = market is nervous.
Bollinger Bands
In plain words: Upper and lower boundaries of normal price movement (20-day average ±2 std devs).
In this dashboard: When price touches the edge, it may bounce back.
Say it like this: "Price hit upper band" = Nifty moved 2 standard deviations above average.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
In plain words: Momentum between 0 (all down) and 100 (all up).
In this dashboard: Below 30 = oversold; above 70 = overbought.
Say it like this: "RSI is 25" = Nifty has moved down hard recently and may bounce.
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
In plain words: Shows whether momentum is speeding up or slowing down.
In this dashboard: Positive histogram = momentum is bullish; negative = bearish.
Say it like this: "MACD turned positive" = upward momentum is building.
Supertrend
In plain words: Shows whether price is in an uptrend or downtrend.
In this dashboard: Green = uptrend; red = downtrend; uses closing prices only.
Say it like this: "Supertrend turned red" = price broke below trend support.
p-value
In plain words: Probability that a pattern works by luck, not skill.
In this dashboard: Lower is better. Below 0.05 = worth watching.
Say it like this: "p-value is 0.03" = only 3% chance this works by luck.
Interview Questions
Why do we care about open interest?
Open interest tells you where traders have skin in the game. High OI at a strike means many traders hold positions there. If Nifty hits that strike at expiry, they all win or lose at once. That can create support or resistance. -
What does put-call ratio tell us?
PCR shows whether traders are more bullish (fewer puts) or bearish (more puts). But it's not predictive by itself—traders can be wrong too. We test it against real outcomes. -
How is max pain calculated?
For each possible expiry price, we calculate total profit/loss on all open calls and puts. Max pain is the price where total loss is greatest. It's a theoretical construct—markets don't always move there, but it's worth watching. -
Why test on only the last 40% of data?
If you train and test on the same data, you'll fool yourself—the pattern will work great on old data but fail on new data (overfitting). We use the first 60% to discover a pattern, then check if it works on new 40% data. -
What are you using Holm correction for?
We test many signals. If you test 10 things against random noise, one will look good by luck (this is the multiple-comparisons problem). Holm correction raises the p-value threshold for each test, so we're less likely to fool ourselves. -
Why are the last 20 days removed?
Recent data is noisy—we don't have all the information yet about what truly happened (actual prices, follow-through). By removing the last 20 days, we avoid overfitting to incomplete observations. -
How is support and resistance calculated?
Support is the strike with the highest put OI (puts protect against downside). Resistance is the strike with the highest call OI (calls bet on upside). These are levels where traders have concentrated their bets. -
